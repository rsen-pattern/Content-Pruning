# The refresh playbook — recycling existing content

Recycling is the easiest and cheapest trick for maximising the impact of your content. Good content makes an impact every time. Recycling your content is the way to maximise value and expand the lifetime — your brand and audience can benefit longer after initial publication.

In other words, you're missing out if you don't recycle. Just make sure you balance new and recycled content.

## The importance of recycling

Get more value for money and expand the lifetime of your content. You don't always need new content; look at what you already have and recycle what's successful. Creating good content is expensive, so why not reuse what you already have?

## What not to do

Before diving in, know what to avoid:

- Don't think you always need new content
- Don't presume everyone has seen your content the first time you published. Generally, 12 to 15% see your LinkedIn post, and SEO needs 6 to 12 months before creating significant traffic. There is nothing wrong with repeating what is good — most people hardly remember what they read yesterday, let alone last week or a month ago.
- Don't go big. Instead, start small with a top five or ten content pieces to refresh and republish
- Don't republish without refreshing
- Don't create a new page. Instead, reuse and optimise from the existing SEO ranking

## The 4-step recycling workflow

1. Audit and identify
2. Refresh and update
3. Republish
4. Schedule the next cycle

### Step 1 — Audit and identify

Five things to do in the first step:

1. **Do an audit based on data.** Check your Google Analytics over the past 12 months.
2. **Check how often people visit the page.** Was the content a one-off success due to a social posting, or does the content continuously pull in traffic via SEO?
3. **Does this content spark your joy?** Do you still like it, and is it representative of your brand? If you don't like it, why would they?
4. **Is it still relevant and applicable to your audience?** Or is the content too outdated to republish? Or is it really linked to a season or specific time of the year?
5. **What was the initial date of publication?** Before you recycle and repost, have a minimum of three months in between.

**Prioritise, focus on your top 5.** Create a top five or ten and focus on this. There's no need to do everything at once, and it's perfectly fine to keep some for the next recycling round.

**Keep it simple.** A practical approach: export the most visited pages over the last twelve months. For the top ten, crosscheck on the unique visitors and repeat visitors. Check if these visitors are spread out over time or clustered in a specific week or month. Narrow down the top ten to the top five; only the pages that are older than three months and have high visitors spread out over time. And if the page is still relevant and you like it. That's your list to update and refresh.

### Step 2 — Refresh and update

Now that you have your 5 or 10 content pages to recycle, it's time to update and refresh the content. Simply republishing without changes will not do the job and will make you look lazy.

**Update the content.** Read, check, and update the content. Adjust and rewrite where needed. Address current trends, events, or innovations in your industry. As the world changes, so should your content. Add and expand the content with a new paragraph to make it even more relevant. Small changes can make a major difference.

**Check the links.** Are the existing links still active and relevant? Is there new content you can link to?

**Address user feedback.** Was there any user feedback or questions you can address in your updated version?

**Rewrite the headline** to make it feel fresh and relevant again, where appropriate.

**Refresh the visual.** Adding a new header visual and updating the visuals in the content is the fastest way to make your content look new. Most recycled content is from roughly six months ago — a different time of the year — and needs an updated visual. In the spring, visuals are lighter, whiter, and crispier than in the fall.

**Don't duplicate.** This one is especially important for SEO and analytics. Don't create a new page; use the existing content page and refresh that. Having two pages that are quite similar doesn't favour your site's search ranking; besides, it makes you look stupid to have the same content twice.

If you do an update, include a disclaimer in the last paragraph to prevent your audience from feeling misled. State that it's updated content, refreshed, repolished, and republished — and why you updated it, especially when it's about AI and similar fast-moving topics. Sometimes put 'updated' in the header when it's relevant to signal that there's new content on this topic.

### Step 3 — Republish

This is when you actually hit the 'publish' button to update the publication date. Use the original page and URL to optimise SEO. A page that already ranks well and has a history will always outperform a new page with the updated content. Using the same page also helps to analyse and audit for your next recycling round.

**Repost on social.** If you repost on social, guaranteed you'll reach new and/or different audiences. Never presume that all of your followers have seen your post the first time. If you reach them for a second time, there's nothing wrong with that.

**Add a disclaimer if needed.** Be upfront and mention that it's updated content. You don't want to mislead your audience and present something new while it's updated content. While most people don't remember what content they read last week, some do, or it will ring a vague bell and provoke a feeling of being misled.

### Step 4 — Schedule the next cycle

Set a reminder of periodic content audits. You can do this quarterly or bi-annually to identify recycling options continuously. The rule of thumb is to have at least three months between reposting content; that's also why a top 5 or top 10 per round works better than doing everything in one go.

Consistency is key, also in recycling.

**Tip for consistency:** plan this in your calendar. Sounds stupid, yet it works. Plan a meeting or reminder in your calendar every three months to check the data and create a new content recycle list. Realistically you'll skip once in a while, so you'll end up recycling content every six months.

## Summary

| Step | Summary |
|---|---|
| 1. Audit and identify | Audit content based on data (Google Analytics, page visits, brand alignment, relevance, and initial publication date). Prioritise and focus on the top 5. Keep data analysis simple. |
| 2. Refresh and update | Update and rewrite content to address current trends and feedback. Refresh visuals to match the current time. Avoid duplicating content; update the existing page. Add disclaimers for updates. |
| 3. Republish | Republish using the original page and URL to optimise SEO. Repost updated content on social media, clearly indicating its updated nature. |
| 4. Schedule the next cycle | Set reminders for periodic audits, ideally every three months. Plan consistent recycling, incorporating it into your regular calendar. |

While recycling content can expand the lifetime of your content, don't forget to create new content. Avoid overdoing recycling. Be upfront when you recycle and balance it with fresh and new content.
